Meetbell for IT administrators

Last updated: 15 October 2026

Someone at your organisation asked to use Meetbell, a free desktop app for Windows (Microsoft Store; a Mac version is on its way) that flies a small animated reminder across the screen before each meeting, with a button to join it. To read their calendar it signs in with Microsoft, and your tenant asks an administrator to approve that once. One approval covers everyone in your organisation.

What it asks for

PermissionTypeWhy
Calendars.Read (Microsoft Graph)DelegatedRead the signed-in person's own upcoming meetings (title, time, organizer, attendees, location and join link) and the list of their calendars, to choose which ones get reminders.
openid, profile, emailDelegatedSign in, and show which account is connected (its name and address, kept on that computer).
offline_accessDelegatedKeep the connection without asking the person to sign in again.

It never writes to calendars, never reads mail or files, has no application (app-only) permissions, and only sees the calendar of the person who signs in.

Where the data goes

  • Meetbell has no server. It talks to Microsoft Graph directly from each person's computer.
  • Meeting details stay on that computer, in memory and in the app's own folder. Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else.
  • Sign-in tokens are stored encrypted by Windows (DPAPI) or the macOS Keychain, and removed when the person signs out.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no tracking. Full details: privacy policy.

How to approve

  1. Open the approval link the person sent you, or this one.
  2. Sign in as a Global Administrator, Cloud Application Administrator or Application Administrator.
  3. Check the permissions and select Accept. The approval link asks for exactly what the app's sign-in asks for, by name (not /.default): Calendars.Read, with openid, profile and email. offline_access needs no administrator: Microsoft grants it with any delegated permission. There is no User.Read: the app takes the person's name and address from the sign-in itself and never calls the profile API.

If your tenant uses the admin consent workflow, people can press "Request approval" on Microsoft's sign-in page instead; the request arrives under Microsoft Entra admin center › Enterprise applications › Admin consent requests, and approving it there is the same approval.

Application (client) ID: 9e4ab8f9-7769-43dc-a076-ad5f9fe0ccc5. You can also review it under Microsoft Entra admin center › Enterprise applications once anyone has tried to sign in.

How to remove it

Microsoft Entra admin center › Enterprise applications › Meetbell › Properties › Delete (or set "Enabled for users to sign in?" to No). People can also sign out inside Meetbell, which deletes their tokens.

Conditional Access

Meetbell's sign-in is an ordinary Microsoft sign-in in the system browser, so your Conditional Access policies apply as they are. Where only managed or compliant devices may sign in, Meetbell tells people that in plain words (from Microsoft's error codes AADSTS53000 to 53003) and points them to classic Outlook on a company PC, or to you; it never tries to work around a policy.

Without approval

On Windows, Meetbell can read classic Outlook on the same PC with no sign-in. For work calendars it doesn't suggest publishing a calendar link: a published link is an address anyone can open, with the meetings' join links in it, so it's offered only behind "Other ways", with a note to check with you first.
