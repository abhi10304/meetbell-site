Privacy policy

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Meetbell is a desktop app that reminds you of your meetings. It is built so that nothing about you leaves your computer except what is needed to show your calendar and the weather, and none of it reaches us: the app has no servers, no accounts, no analytics and no advertising. Meetbell is made by Abhishek Johri, an independent developer in India.

What the app reads, and where it goes

WhatWhyWhere it goes
Your calendar events (title, time, meeting link, location, organizer and attendee addresses) To fly a reminder before each meeting, open the meeting, mark VIP meetings and write the "running late" message Read directly from Microsoft 365 / Outlook.com, Google Calendar, iCloud or another CalDAV calendar (Fastmail, Yahoo, Zoho, GMX, WEB.DE, Nextcloud, Synology…), a calendar (.ics) link you add, classic Outlook on this computer, or (on a Mac) the calendars in Apple's Calendar app. Meetbell only reads them: Microsoft and Google grant read-only access, and an app password or calendar link is used only to read. Never sent anywhere else.
The last meetings read from each calendar (title, time, meeting link, location, organizer and attendees; not the invitation text) So reminders still come right after the computer starts, while you're offline, or while a calendar can't be reached Kept on your computer in cache.json in the app's folder, for 24 hours at most; a calendar you remove is taken out of it. Never sent anywhere.
Sign-in tokens for Microsoft or Google (only if you connect those) To keep reading your calendar without signing in again Stored on your computer, encrypted by Windows (DPAPI) or the macOS Keychain. Sent only to Microsoft or Google. Removed when you sign out, and on Windows when you uninstall the app (see Your control).
Your account's name and e-mail address (only if you sign in with Microsoft or Google) To show which account is connected, put your first name on the boarding pass, and recognise meetings you declined Stored on your computer, encrypted with the sign-in. Never sent anywhere.
Your computer's user name (only when no Microsoft or Google sign-in gives a name) To put a first name on the boarding pass and the flight-log picture Read on your computer when the pass or the picture is drawn; not stored and never sent anywhere. The picture leaves your computer only if you share it yourself, and "Hide my name" leaves the name off.
The user name and app password of an iCloud or other CalDAV calendar (only if you add one) To read that calendar Stored on your computer, encrypted like the sign-ins. Sent only to that calendar's own server, over an encrypted (https) connection; never to another website, even if the server points somewhere else. Removed when you remove the calendar.
Calendar (.ics) links you add To read those calendars Stored on your computer, encrypted like the sign-ins (they work like passwords). Sent only to the calendar's own server. Never written to the app's log.
The calendars on your Mac (only if you allow it) To read the events in Apple's Calendar app macOS asks you first, and you can turn it off in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Calendars. Read on your Mac; nothing is sent anywhere.
Your work e-mail domain (only if you use "Ask IT" or the admin-approval link in the Calendars window) To find your organisation's Microsoft tenant, so the approval link goes to the right place Sent to Microsoft's public sign-in service (login.microsoftonline.com), which answers with the organisation's ID. Nothing else is sent.
The city you choose for the weather (its name and map position) To show weather and air quality in the widget While you search for a city, what you type (and the app's language, for the place names) is sent to Open-Meteo's place search; afterwards only the city's map position (latitude and longitude) is sent to Open-Meteo, a free weather service, from your computer. No account and nothing about you is sent; like any website, Open-Meteo sees your internet (IP) address.
Whether an app is using your microphone or camera right now, whether a full-screen app is running, and whether Windows' "Do not disturb" is on (Windows) To make reminders small and silent while you're on a call, sharing your screen, presenting or not to be disturbed, and to notice that you joined a meeting from another app Checked on your computer when a reminder is about to fly and, while "Notice when I join from another app" is on (the default), a few times from 2 minutes before to 10 minutes after the start of each online meeting you didn't join from Meetbell. Only whether the microphone or camera is in use is read: Meetbell never hears or sees anything. If it was in use, today's meeting log marks that meeting as joined. Nothing is sent.
Your settings, frequent-flyer miles, the reminders you add, today's meeting log, a count-only summary of past days (how many meetings, how many on time; no titles or people) and the recurring series you mute To remember your choices, show your boarding pass and landing report, make your flight-log picture, and stop reminders for a muted series Stored on your computer in the app's folder. Today's meeting log keeps the meetings' titles until the next day: once Meetbell runs on a later day, only that day's counts are kept, and the summary keeps the counts of up to 400 days. A muted series keeps its title until you unmute it. Never sent anywhere. The flight-log picture is made on your computer and leaves it only if you copy, save or post it yourself.
A log of what the app did and crash reports To find out what went wrong if you ask for help Kept on your computer only, in the app's folder. Calendar links, e-mail addresses, tokens and passwords are taken out before anything is written, and meeting titles are never written. Nothing is uploaded; "Copy diagnostics" copies a short report to your clipboard for you to send yourself.

When you press Running late?, the app opens a Teams chat or an e-mail draft addressed to the organizer. Nothing is sent until you press Send in that app yourself.

When a reminder can't be shown as a plane (your screen is locked, you're away, or the plane couldn't fly), the app may also show it as a system notification. On the lock screen, and while you're away, that notification says "Meeting" instead of the meeting's title.

Google user data

Meetbell's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google Calendar data is used only on your computer: to show your reminders, the widget, your boarding pass and landing report, and to address a “running late” message that you choose to send. It is not stored on any server, not used for advertising, not sold, not used to train AI models, and not read by people.

What the app does not do

  • It does not collect, sell or share personal information.
  • It does not track you, show ads, or use analytics or crash-reporting services.
  • It never writes to your calendar.
  • It does not record your screen, microphone or camera.

Your control

  • Disconnect a calendar from the app's Calendars window; its tokens or app password are deleted (and the tokens revoked where the provider supports it), and its meetings leave cache.json.
  • Turn the weather off, or remove your city, in Settings › General.
  • Uninstalling Meetbell from the Microsoft Store removes everything it stored. The Windows installer removes your sign-ins, calendar links and cache.json; your settings, miles, reminders, today's meeting log and muted series stay in %APPDATA%\Meetbell for a reinstall, and you can delete that folder (the portable version removes nothing). On a Mac, remove your calendars in Meetbell before deleting the app; what remains is in ~/Library/Containers/com.meetbell.app, plus the app's “Meetbell Safe Storage” key in Keychain Access.

Children

The app is a work tool and is not directed at children.
