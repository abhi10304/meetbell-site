Privacy policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026

Meetbell is a desktop app that reminds you of your meetings. It is built so that nothing about you leaves your computer except what is needed to show your calendar and the weather, and none of it reaches us: the app has no servers, no accounts, no analytics and no advertising. Meetbell is made by Abhishek Johri, an independent developer in India.

The answer is a version number and a link. The website has no analytics; like any website, its host, GitHub, sees your internet (IP) address. Turn it off in Settings › General.

When you press Running late?, the app opens a Teams chat or an e-mail draft addressed to the organizer. Nothing is sent until you press Send in that app yourself.

When you press Save video (Share › Flight video), a short clip (about 8 seconds) is made on your computer and saved as a file in your Videos folder (on the Mac App Store version, where you choose): a sample plane with the title you type, never a meeting from your calendar. It leaves your computer only if you share it yourself.

Tell me what to improve on the landing report opens an e-mail draft to the developer with only the app's version in the subject: no meetings, calendar or computer details. Nothing is sent until you press Send yourself.

When a reminder can't be shown as a plane (your screen is locked, you're away, or the plane couldn't fly), the app may also show it as a system notification. On the lock screen, and while you're away, that notification says "Meeting" instead of the meeting's title.

Google user data

Meetbell's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google Calendar data is used only on your computer: to show your reminders, the widget, your boarding pass and landing report, and to address a “running late” message that you choose to send. It is not stored on any server, not used for advertising, not sold, not used to train AI models, and not read by people. It is protected as described in How your data is protected.

How your data is protected

Meetbell treats your calendar events, sign-in tokens, app passwords and calendar links as sensitive data, including all Google user data, and protects them with these mechanisms:

  • No servers, no copies with us. Your data is processed only on your computer. Meetbell has no servers or database, so the developer never receives, stores or has access to your calendar, sign-ins or passwords.
  • Encrypted in transit. Everything Meetbell reads from Google, Microsoft, iCloud and other calendar servers travels over encrypted HTTPS (TLS) connections, directly between your computer and that service. Calendar links that start with http:// or webcal:// are changed to https://. An app password is sent only to its own calendar server over HTTPS, and never passed on when a server redirects elsewhere.
  • Encrypted at rest. Sign-in tokens and app passwords are stored encrypted with your operating system's key store: Windows Data Protection (DPAPI), tied to your Windows account, or the macOS Keychain. They are never saved unencrypted: if that encryption isn't available, Meetbell doesn't save them. Calendar links are encrypted the same way wherever the key store is available.
  • Least access. Meetbell asks Google only for your name and e-mail address and for read-only access to your calendar events and list of calendars (calendar.events.readonly and calendar.calendarlist.readonly), and Microsoft only for read-only calendar access (Calendars.Read). It can't change, send or delete anything. If you untick calendar access on Google's consent page, Meetbell gives the sign-in back to Google at once.
  • Secure sign-in. You sign in on Google's or Microsoft's own page in your browser (OAuth 2.0 with PKCE), so Meetbell never sees your Google or Microsoft password.
  • Kept briefly, on your computer only. Meeting details are kept only in the app's folder in your own user profile, protected by your operating system's account permissions: the last meetings read from each calendar for 24 hours at most, today's meeting titles until the next day, and a series you mute keeps its title until you unmute it. Disconnecting a calendar deletes its tokens or app password, revokes the tokens where the provider supports it, and removes its meetings from the cache.
  • An isolated app. Meetbell's windows run in Chromium's sandbox with a strict content security policy. They show only Meetbell's own pages and can't send anything to the internet themselves.
  • Nothing sensitive in logs. Calendar links, e-mail addresses, tokens and passwords are removed before anything is written to the app's log, and meeting titles are never written. Nothing is uploaded.
  • No human access. No person, including the developer, reads your Google user data. It is never sold, shared, or used for advertising or to train AI models.

What the app does not do

  • It does not collect, sell or share personal information.
  • It does not track you, show ads, or use analytics or crash-reporting services.
  • It never writes to your calendar.
  • It does not record your screen, microphone or camera.

Your control

  • Disconnect a calendar from the app's Calendars window; its tokens or app password are deleted (and the tokens revoked where the provider supports it), and its meetings leave cache.json.
  • Turn the weather off, or remove your city, in Settings › General.
  • Turn the version check off in Settings › General (in the versions downloaded from the website).
  • Uninstalling Meetbell from the Microsoft Store removes everything it stored. The Windows installer removes your sign-ins, calendar links and cache.json; your settings, miles, reminders, today's meeting log and muted series stay in %APPDATA%\Meetbell for a reinstall, and you can delete that folder (the portable version removes nothing). On a Mac, remove your calendars in Meetbell before deleting the app; what remains is in ~/Library/Containers/com.meetbell.app, plus the app's “Meetbell Safe Storage” key in Keychain Access.
